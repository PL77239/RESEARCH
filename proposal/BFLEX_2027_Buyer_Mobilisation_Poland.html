Buyer Mobilisation Proposal – Poland

7th Bharat Footwear & Leather Expo (BFLEX) · 15–16 March 2027 · New Delhi, India

Prepared for: Council for Leather Exports (CLE)

Prepared by: Poland Importers – Trading and Market Research · poland-importers.com

Date: 22 October 2026

1. Summary

We propose to build a qualified delegation of Polish buyers covering three product groups:

Group Scope Indicative quota*
A. Footwear Leather footwear for men, women and children, including casual, formal and safety footwear 6 buyers
B. Leather accessories Bags, small leather goods (wallets, card holders), belts, gloves, travel goods 4 buyers
C. Leather apparel Leather and suede jackets, coats, garments 2–3 buyers

* The final number will follow the allocation confirmed by CLE. For each group we will submit about 1.5–2 times the quota in profiles. This gives CLE a real choice and leaves a pre-approved reserve list.

Our approach has three principles:

  1. Quality over quantity. We only propose companies that import, or are ready to import, in commercial volumes. We only propose delegates who have purchasing authority.
  2. CLE decides. No buyer is invited, and no participation is promised, before CLE has approved the buyer's profile in writing. Every candidate is told at first contact that participation depends on CLE approval.
  3. Each buyer arrives with a clear sourcing need. Every approved buyer comes to New Delhi with defined product needs. CLE can share these needs with exhibitors in advance, so meetings at the show are pre-matched rather than random.

Final approved list: Monday, 30 November 2026.

2.3 Who will travel – delegate requirements

  • The delegate is a decision-maker: owner or CEO, purchasing or sourcing director, or head buyer or category manager with budget responsibility. Junior staff, designers without purchasing authority and external consultants are not accepted.
  • One delegate per company. A second delegate is possible only for large chains (50 or more stores, or a major e-commerce business) and only with separate CLE approval.
  • The delegate speaks working English.
  • The delegate holds a valid passport (at least 6 months' validity after the event) and agrees to apply for an Indian visa on time.
  • The delegate commits in writing to attend both show days, take part in the pre-arranged B2B meetings and give feedback after the event (see Section 4.5).

3. Selection criteria

3.1 Must-have criteria (pass or fail)

A company that fails any of these criteria is not submitted to CLE.

  1. A Polish company registered in KRS (National Court Register) or CEIDG (register of sole traders), with a valid VAT number, trading for at least 2 years.
  2. An active business in footwear, leather accessories or leather apparel that fits the BFLEX exhibitor categories.
  3. Direct import of the relevant products from outside the EU, or a documented plan and ability to start importing (logistics, financing, customs).
  4. Real purchasing volume. Indicative minimums:
  5. footwear: about 5,000 pairs a year
  6. accessories: about 3,000 pieces a year
  7. apparel: about 500 pieces a year

CLE may apply lower thresholds for niche or premium buyers. 5. A concrete sourcing need for the 2027/28 seasons (product types, materials, price points, quantities). 6. The delegate meets the requirements in Section 2.3. 7. The company has no unresolved trade disputes, insolvency or restructuring proceedings. We check this in public registers.

4. Process: from long list to approved delegation

4.1 Step-by-step workflow

Long list (120–150 companies)
   ↓  desk screening against the must-have criteria
Screened list (60–80)
   ↓  qualification calls with decision-makers (structured questionnaire, Annex B)
Qualified candidates (30–40)
   ↓  scoring, documents, signed declaration of intent
Profiles submitted to CLE in 3 batches (about 1.5–2× the quota)
   ↓  CLE review: Approved / Rejected / Query / Waitlist
Approved delegation + approved reserve list   →   30 November 2026
  1. Long list. We build the long list from our own database of Polish importers and buyers, built over more than 25 years of trade work. We add public registers (KRS, CEIDG), trade-fair exhibitor and visitor lists, industry associations and e-commerce market data. Each company is assigned to Group A, B or C.
  2. Desk screening. We check registration, years of trading, financial data where available, sales channels, product ranges and price points. Companies that fail any must-have criterion are removed.
  3. Qualification calls. Senior consultants talk directly with the decision-maker, in Polish. They present BFLEX, the Indian supply base, CLE's hosted-buyer terms and the approval requirement, then complete the structured questionnaire (Annex B).
  4. Documentation. For every qualified candidate we collect:
  5. the completed profile form
  6. a company registration extract
  7. links to the website, online shop or store locator
  8. a signed Declaration of Intent (Section 4.5)
  9. the delegate's written GDPR consent to share personal data with CLE in India
  10. Scoring and recommendation. We score each candidate internally and write a short recommendation.
  11. Submission to CLE in three batches (Section 4.3).
  12. Invitations. After CLE approval, CLE issues formal invitations. We do not issue invitations ourselves.

4.2 How profiles are shared with CLE

  • Buyer profile (PDF, 1–2 pages per buyer). A standard template (Annex A) used for every buyer, so profiles are easy to compare.
  • Master shortlist tracker (Excel). One row per buyer with the group, buyer type, score, category (A or B), our recommendation, and a CLE decision column.
  • Supporting documents. Registration extract, signed Declaration of Intent and GDPR consent, filed per buyer.
  • Secure shared folder. On a platform chosen by CLE, or provided by us, with access limited to named CLE officers. Each batch is announced by email with a short summary.
  • CLE decision codes:
  • APPROVED: the buyer is confirmed for invitation.
  • REJECTED: we inform the company politely and give no further commitment.
  • QUERY: CLE needs more information. We answer within 2 working days.
  • WAITLIST: the buyer is approved as a reserve and can replace an approved buyer who drops out.
  • Weekly status call (30 minutes, online) with the CLE contact person during November.
  • All personal data is processed under the GDPR. Delegates give explicit consent to the transfer of their data to CLE in India for the purposes of the event.

4.5 Buyer Declaration of Intent

Before a profile is sent to CLE, each candidate signs a short declaration confirming that they will:

  • attend both days of BFLEX and the B2B meetings scheduled by CLE
  • not transfer the invitation to another person or company without CLE approval
  • inform us immediately if they cannot travel
  • complete the post-event feedback and the 3- and 6-month follow-ups
  • accept that participation is subject to CLE approval and to CLE's hosted-buyer terms

5. Risk control

Risk How we manage it
Buyers who come mainly for the trip Must-have criteria, phone verification, registry checks, scoring and the signed declaration
Last-minute cancellations Approved waitlist, acceptance within 7 days of the invitation, reconfirmation 2 weeks and 1 week before the show
Visa or travel delays Early checklist, e-Visa guidance, applications filed early
Buyer needs do not match exhibitors A sourcing brief for each buyer, shared with CLE for pre-matching
Delegation dominated by one group Quotas per group and a balanced mix of buyer types (chains, brands, wholesalers, e-commerce)
Personal data protection GDPR-compliant consent and a restricted-access shared folder

6. Success indicators

  • At least 90% of approved buyers attend.
  • At least 8 pre-arranged B2B meetings per buyer during the show.
  • At least 50% of buyers request samples or quotations within 3 months.
  • Trial or confirmed orders are tracked and reported at 3 and 6 months.

Annex B – Qualification call questionnaire (summary)

  1. What share of your range is made of leather? Which product groups and price segments?
  2. Which countries do you import from today? Do you import directly or through agents?
  3. What are your annual volumes per product group? What MOQ per style can you accept?
  4. Which products are you looking to source for AW 2027 and SS 2028? What are your target prices?
  5. Have you worked with Indian suppliers? If so, what was your experience? If not, why not?
  6. Who in the company approves samples and places orders? Will that person travel?
  7. Can you attend both days, 15–16 March 2027? Do you have a valid passport?
  8. Which compliance requirements apply to your purchases (REACH, safety standards, audits)?
